Following Google's layoffs affecting open source maintainers, the author examines OpenTofu's development velocity six months after forking from Terraform. The analysis compares pledged resources against actual community contributions.

## The Pledges

When OpenTofu launched, the project highlighted "18 FTEs pledged by 4 companies to work full time on OpenTofu for at least 5 years." Additionally, over 800 individuals and 160+ companies pledged vague support, primarily offering "development; open-source community efforts" without specifics.

The author initially expected these commitments would enable OpenTofu to outpace HashiCorp's development efforts.

## Reality Check

**GitHub contributor analysis reveals:**

- OpenTofu main repository: Only ~5 active contributors currently, far below the 18 promised FTEs
- Terraform (HashiCorp): Significantly more contributors demonstrating higher investment
- OpenTofu Registry: Similar pattern—initial contributors dropped off after months

The author notes that comparing commit counts shows HashiCorp invests substantially more resources than the entire OpenTofu community combined.

## Contributor Burnout Pattern

Monthly pulse graphs show about 6 people contributing meaningfully to OpenTofu versus 9 for Terraform. The author questions whether developers posting only "a couple of commits in a month and a half" qualify as full-time equivalents or merely drive-by contributors.

## Conclusion

The author expresses disappointment (though unsurprised) at the gap between pledges and actual involvement. Similar patterns appeared in their own AutoSpotting project, suggesting this represents a broader open source challenge where companies pledge support without sustained commitment.
